Project Phase 1: LeRobot Open Source Policy Training

Task: Pick up a 1.5in cube and place it in an open top box. A given policy has a successful episode if it grasps the block and places it into the box on the first try. If it misses and then tries to re-grip this is an unsuccessful episode, regardless of the success of the second try. During testing I removed the block from the environment immediately after episode failure, preventing this case entirely.

I trained 2 policies, each off of 50 episodes of demonstration using LeRoot Hugging Face toolset.

Policy 1 has a success rate of 32%. It experiences significant issues with depth perception due to the camera mount location. This issue was observed through the common failure mode of the arm grasping directly behind the block.

Policy 2 (V3) uses a new camera mount that provides a better overhead view of the workspace. This new view both minimizes the need for depth perception and also puts more of the workspace into frame, allowing for a larger array of actions. The success rate of this policy is 68%.

Note that the success rate of this entire dataset is 58%, but for the last 9 trials I was experimenting with edge cases that were not part of training. The success rate of the policy without these edge cases is 68%.

Discovery

I conducted four 1 hour discovery interviews with individuals across three business units. The interviews held a conversational format using the Jobs-to-be-Done framework to understand current workflows and pain points. The goal for this project was to design around how our sales reps actually work, not how we assumed they would use the new Agentforce platform.

Designing & prototyping

Throughout the data analysis and prototyping process I used Claude extensively. I added the interview transcripts to the context window and used Claude to pull key metrics, such as the impact of pain points voiced during interviews.

Shared jobs across business units surfaced through discovery, as shown in the Venn-Diagram (Figure 2.1). I mapped these shared jobs onto Agentforce solutions that would maximuse efficiency for each job identified (Figure 2.2).

A1: in focus

After identifying the subagents to be designed, I honed in on subagent A1: Account intelligence. This agent will provide an account summary right on the salesforce landing page, pulling the specific metrics that the end users voiced in discovery in oder to give the sales reps an automated account brief specifically designed for the needs of their business unit. The discovery interviews laid bare that an agent of this nature would save each sales rep upwards of 30 minutes before each account contact point.

In addition to the efficiency gains that the Account Intelligence agent provides, this agent also holds the potential to improve the quality of the data interpretation that the sales reps walk into customer meetings with. With the agent

As part of creating an optimized account brief view, additional agents will pull service history of that customer’s devices, and data from other systems outside of salesforce.

UX rules identified

Push, don’t pull: individuals made it clear that the most impactful and useful tools are the ones that get automatically pushed to their dashboards.

One agent, three views: each of the BUs requested different views into the same core data points, so a single agent with individualized sub-agents should be utilized, as they will all be grounded in the same data

Prototyping

Using Claude Design I was able to create elaborate prototypes in HTML for the end users to get an accurate understanding of what the user experience will be of the shipped agent. This efficient prototyping process allowed me to create the individualized view for each of the business units.

Validation sessions

Returned to the same interview participants with the HTML prototypes for 30 minute feedback sessions on design utility. These sessions resulted in further design criteria for the next iteration of prototypes.

Lasting Value

Discovery interviews: These will act as a longstanding source of truth after the end of my internship experience.

Agent architecture: I created a handoff deck with a complete overview of the agent architecture and rollout plan, and facilitated handoff the next person that will be spearheading this initiative.
